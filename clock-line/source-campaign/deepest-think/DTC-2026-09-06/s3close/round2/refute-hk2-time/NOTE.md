## 2. F1: what field the window numbers belong to, and what Lemma 7.2 actually licenses

### 2.1 The model

hk2 section 8(c) and `k3_bound.py` evaluate the majorant on `eta_lam = eta_0 o T_lam^{-1}`,
the scalar transported by the pure coaxial strain `T_lam(y,z) = (lam y, lam^{-2} z)`, with
`lam` the axis e-fold count.  Its gradient is `T_lam^{-T} grad eta_0`, i.e. the `y`-components
of `grad eta_0` shrink by `1/lam` and the `z`-component grows by `lam^2`.  This is the exact
inviscid transport under a *spatially constant* strain.  The Navier-Stokes field is
`eta_0 o Phi_t^{-1}` smoothed by diffusion, with `Phi_t` the true flow map, whose Jacobian
is `T_lam` only to the accuracy GAP T would provide (`lower/prove-lagrangian` section 4(2);
`s3close/assembly` section 2, BLOCK 2 and 3).  hk2 says so (section 7, section 12 item 4).

### 2.2 What hk2's own propagation lemma gives

hk2's Lemma 7.2, quoted:

    |grad eta(x,t)|  <= e^{int Gamma} E[|grad eta_0(Y_t)|]
    |Hess eta(x,t)|  <= e^{2 int Gamma} ( E[|Hess eta_0(Y_t)|] + K2 int E|grad eta| )

with `Y` the backward diffusion.  This is an **isotropic** bound: it multiplies the *initial*
gradient at the backward point by `e^{int Gamma}`, and it knows nothing about the direction
of `grad eta_0`.  Dropping diffusion (`Y_t -> T_lam^{-1} x`), the tail, and the feedback term,
it licenses, as inputs to Corollary 4.3,

    TV density      lam^2 |grad eta_0|(T^{-1} x')          instead of   |T^{-T} grad eta_0|(T^{-1} x')
    ball sup grad   lam^2 sup |grad eta_0| over the label ball
    ball sup Hess   lam^4 sup ||Hess eta_0||_F over the label ball
    local grad      lam^2 |grad eta_0|(label point)

(`e^{int Gamma} = lam^2` is V-b's `Gamma = 2a(0,t)`, `int Gamma = 2 log lam`; F6 below.)

`r2_propagation.py` runs the same majorant, same `d`-optimisation, with these inputs:

| `lam` | model (hk2) | Lemma-7.2-licensed | ratio | `Lambda_5` ratio | `Lambda_4` ratio | licensed `d*` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.25 | 107.68 | **136.12** | 1.264 | 1.550 | 1.429 | 0.1427 |
| 1.50 | 161.76 | **243.75** | 1.507 | 1.805 | 1.842 | 0.1175 |

Where the difference comes from: on the ball the two gradient sups nearly coincide
(model / licensed `= 0.946`, `0.962`: the equatorial layer's `z`-gradient dominates there, and
for a `z`-directed gradient `|T^{-T} v| = lam^2 |v|`), but the far field is `r`-dominated
(`eta_P = -M sgn(z)/r`), where `|T^{-T} v| = |v|/lam` against the licensed `lam^2 |v|`, a factor
`lam^3 = 3.375`; the kernel integrals therefore rise by `1.8` and the licensed Hessian ball sup
is `lam^4`-scaled (model / licensed `= 0.48`).

So: **hk2's Lemma 7.2 does not deliver hk2's table.**  The table's smaller numbers use the
anisotropy of `T_lam^{-T}`, which is a property of the model's Jacobian, not of anything
Lemma 7.2 proves.  To keep 161.8 one needs the true backward Jacobian `(D Phi_t)^{-T}` to be
`T_lam^{-T}(1 + O(c/L))` pointwise on the support of `|D eta|`, i.e. a Jacobian-level form of
GAP T, plus its insertion inside the Feynman-Kac expectation.  Neither is in the record.

### 2.3 The growth factor itself (F6)

Lemma 7.1 says `int_0^tau Gamma = 2 log(3/2)`, "exactly 9/4".  Source (V-b section 1):
`Gamma(t) = 2a(0,t)` is a **standing hypothesis**, and V-b's own self-attack item 8 admits an
`O(1)` error in `Gamma`.  The record's proved statement is Theorem Gamma
(`write/L3v-and-gamma-bound` B6, corrected by its refuter section 2.4):
`Gamma <= 2a(0,t) + C' M` on the slab, `C' <= 152` unconditionally, `120` with the computed
`V_lam`, `77` odd-only, **and only for the model field `eta_lam`** (assembly BLOCK 3).
With `tau = theta_max/(M L)`:

    e^{int Gamma} = (9/4) e^{C' theta_max / L}

| `C'` | `L = 10` | 40 | 160 | 640 | 2560 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (V-b hypothesis) | 2.25 | 2.25 | 2.25 | 2.25 | 2.25 |
| 76.11 | 600 | 9.09 | 3.19 | 2.46 | 2.30 |
| 119.33 | 1.43e4 | 20.1 | 3.89 | 2.58 | 2.33 |
| 151.15 | 1.48e5 | 36.0 | 4.50 | 2.68 | 2.35 |

(`r3_results.json`, `e_int_Gamma`.)  The Hessian input carries the square.  This is the
`L`-dependence the honest chain has: not `log(R/rho0)` in the kernel integrals (hk2 is right
that there is none), but `e^{C' theta_max/L}` from the strain's `O(M)` remainder, vanishing
only as `L -> infinity`.  At `L = 10`, where every hk2 number is quoted, the licensed factor on
the gradient is between `600` and `1.5e5`.

### 2.4 Diffusion and the TV measure

hk2 propagates the TV density (2.1) only by naming GAP T (section 7, last sentence).  GAP T is
about the flow map; diffusion is a separate mechanism and it is not small on this window.  At
`lam = 3/2` the equatorial layer, of label half-thickness `w rho = 0.2 rho`, is thinned by
`lam^{-2}` to `0.0749 rho0`; the diffusion length is `sqrt(nu tau) = rho0 sin(delta) sqrt(theta_max/L)
= 0.0354 rho0` at `L = 10` (ratio `0.47`), `0.0177` at `L = 40`.  The strained layer's gradient
(which is what dominates the ball sup, section 2.2) is therefore `O(1)`-modified by diffusion at
`L = 10`, and the TV measure that enters `Lambda_5` near the layer with it.  The correct
propagation of `|D eta|` under drift-diffusion is `d/dt int |grad eta| <= int (||grad b|| + div_5 b)
|grad eta|` (Kato), and `div_5 b = 2a` (hk2 Lemma 3.1), so the *global* TV grows by up to
`e^{int (Gamma + 2a)} = lam^4 = 5.06` under the V-b hypothesis; the weighted integrals `Lambda_p`
need the local version, which no one has written.  This is a remaining gap, not a wrong number.

## 4. F3: the Gaussian tail of Lemma 7.2 (`r3`, `r4`)

hk2 section 7: "the tail is Gaussian with `d^2/(8 nu tau) = d^2 L/(8 (rho0 delta)^2 theta_max)`;
at `d = 0.38 rho0`, `delta = 7.5 deg`, that exponent is `1.444 L`, `5.6e-7` at `L = 10`".

The formula reproduces (`14.434` at `L = 10` with `sin delta`, `14.35` with `delta` in radians;
`check_constants.py` line 121 stores `1.444` as a transcribed formula, no probability is
computed anywhere in `k1`...`k9`).  Two errors in reading it:

1. `x = d^2/(8 nu tau) = (d/2)^2/(2 nu tau)` is the **`chi^2_5` threshold**, not the exponent.
   For a 5-D Brownian motion with generator `nu Lap` (per-coordinate variance `2 nu t`),
   `P(|Z_tau| > d/2) = P(chi^2_5 > x)`, which is `e^{-x/2}` times a polynomial.  At `d = 0.38`,
   `L = 10`: `P = 1.31e-2`, not `5.4e-7` (`scipy.stats.chi2.sf`, `r3_results.json`).
2. The tables do not use `d = 0.38`.  They use `d* = 0.1662, 0.2029, 0.1672` (hk2 section 8(c)),
   and Lemma 7.2 splits at `d/2`.  At `L = 10`:

| `d` | `x` | `e^{-x}` (hk2's reading) | `P(chi^2_5 > x)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.38 | 14.43 | 5.4e-7 | **1.3e-2** |
| 0.2029 | 4.12 | 1.6e-2 | **0.53** |
| 0.1672 | 2.79 | 6.1e-2 | **0.73** |
| 0.1662 | 2.76 | 6.3e-2 | **0.74** |

The tail term is `||grad^k eta_0||_inf P`.  Over `rho >= rho0/2` (excluding the origin defect
of F4), `sup ||Hess eta_0||_F = 301` (at the taper, `phi = 179.7 deg`, `rho = 1.11`) and
`sup |grad eta_0| = 13.8`.  At `L = 10`, `d* = 0.1672`: the Hessian tail term is `220` against
the ball value `72` the table uses, and the gradient tail term `10.1` against the ball value
`10.5`.  So at `L = 10` Lemma 7.2 does not reduce to the ball sup; the tail dominates.

`L` needed for `P < 1e-2 / 1e-3 / 1e-6`: at `d = 0.1672`: `54 / 73 / 128`; at `d = 0.2029`:
`37 / 50 / 87`; at `d = 0.38`: `10.5 / 14.2 / 24.9`.  hk2's "5.6e-7 at `L = 10`" is the
`d = 0.38` figure read with the wrong exponent; with the tables' own `d*` the tail is `O(1)` at
`L = 10` and small only from `L ~ 50 ... 75`.

This does not change the `L -> infinity` structure.  It does mean that every number in hk2's
section 8(c) table is, as a bound on the true field, unsupported at the `L = 10` it is quoted at
even before F1's factor is applied.

## 5. F4: the datum at the origin (`r4`)

`Theta(rho) = (1/2)[tanh((rho - 1)/w0) - tanh((rho - R)/w1)]` with `w0 = 0.25`:
`Theta(0) = (1/2)(1 - tanh 4) = 3.3535e-4`.  Then near the origin
`eta_0 = omega/r -> -Theta(0) tanh(sin phi/sin delta) tanh(cos phi/w)/r`, and along `phi -> 0`
this is `-Theta(0) tanh(cos phi/w)/(rho sin delta)`.  Measured at `phi = 1 deg`:
`rho eta_0 = -0.002997, -0.002766, -0.002658, -0.002595` at `rho = 0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.002`,
converging to the predicted `-0.002569`; `||Hess eta_0||_F = 2.6e7` at `rho = 0.002`.

Consequences: (D1)'s `|eta|` bounded is false for (D-B) as literally defined;
`||eta_0||_inf = infinity` makes Lemma 7.1's second clause vacuous; the "global `K2`" of
section 8(d) is `+infinity` at the origin for (D-B) too (coefficient `Theta(0)/sin delta =
2.6e-3`, exceeding `M/rho0^2` for `rho < 0.051 rho0`).  None of this touches the tube: the
origin is `0.84 rho0` from the tracked point and its contribution to `Lambda_p` is integrable
(`rho'^4 drho' / rho'^2`).  Repair: set `Theta = 0` for `rho < rho0/2` (which `dcommon.py`'s grid
presumably never resolves anyway).  Filed because hk2's section 8(d) sentence is the one that
answers the V-b refuter's Finding 4 (the coupling lemma needs the global sup), and it is wrong.

## 8. Remaining gap

To turn hk2's window table into a theorem about the Navier-Stokes field one needs, in this
order: (a) Theorem Gamma for the true field (BLOCK 3) or a substitute for `Gamma = 2a(0,t)`;
(b) a Jacobian-level GAP T, `(D Phi_t)^{-T} = T_lam^{-T}(1 + O(c/L))` on the support of
`|D eta|`, inserted inside Lemma 7.2's Feynman-Kac representation so that the anisotropic
factor is licensed; (c) the local propagation of the TV measure `|D eta(t)|` under
drift-diffusion for the weighted integrals `Lambda_4, Lambda_5` (section 2.4); (d) Lemma 7.2's
tail at the `d*` actually used, which forces `L >~ 54 ... 74` before the tail is `< 1e-2`;
(e) the sup over the tube rather than over the trajectory (section 3).  Without (a)-(b) the
number that stands is `243.7` (isotropic, `Gamma = 2a(0)` assumed); with the record's proved
`Gamma` it is `L`-dependent and only approaches a constant as `L -> infinity`.
