## 1. Verdict

The three trusted statement files certify sound, non-trivial weakenings of the papers' headline theorems, not the theorems as printed. No loophole was found: every cheap object (zero solution, bounded-vorticity solution, already-singular solution, fderiv-is-zero or derivWithin junk values, vacuous Bochner integrals, `eLpNorm.toReal` of infinity, trivial filters, empty competitor classes) is excluded by an accompanying clause, and the Challenge definition layers are text-identical to the library originals. What each certificate leaves out: **euler-blowup** (`Challenge.lean:56-66`) drops everything axisymmetric and geometric in Theorem 1.1 (`euler.txt:60-78`): the universal r0 (Solution fixes r₀ = 1, `Solution.lean:21`) and z0, axisymmetry of u0 and f, nonzero swirl, zero meridional initial velocity, torus support of u0, f, Γ and ω (the certificate has compact support of u0 and a ball for f, `Challenge.lean:12`), the bound on Γ, u^r, u^z, and ‖∇Γ‖∞ → ∞; what remains (classical blow-up under a force with every mixed derivative bounded up to T, from smooth compactly supported finite-energy data, ∫‖ω‖∞ = ∞, uniqueness in the finite-energy Lipschitz class) is the theorem's substance and is rendered faithfully. **boussinesq-blowup** (`Challenge.lean:67-79`) certifies blow-up for *some* odd compactly supported datum, for every κ > 0, with no Biot–Savart law, no pressure gauge and no support ball for the state; it is not the paper's Theorem 1.1 (`boussinesq.txt:105-120`, fixed datum (1.4)), and the paper's own AI statement (`boussinesq.txt:366-367`) says the Lean-verified solution is "not this one". **affinecore** (`Challenge.lean:232-250`) is the project that states Theorem 1.1's form (explicit datum thetaIn with N₀ = ⌈√A₀/2⌉₊, u 0 = 0, buoyancy 1, log-kernel Biot–Savart, p(t,0) = 0, fixed ball for θ, curl u and forces, full gradient limit, vorticity limsup, uniqueness); its gaps are small (no support ball for u(t) itself, forces on [0,T) rather than C_c^∞(R²×R), no pressure uniqueness), but its comparator run is recorded as pending and its maintainer read has not happened. Provenance is the weak side: the IPM announcement's SHA-256 placeholders are unfilled (`ipm.txt:107-109`), so none of the three directories is bound to what was announced, and the euler yaml's "author-verified" note leans on a divergence list that does not record the dropped geometry.

## 3. Findings refuted or downgraded (do not re-raise)

- E-02 was filed MINOR ("not recorded as a divergence"); both votes downgraded to NOTE because the Lean form is stated in README 16-17 and yaml 7-8, 80-81 and the two clauses are equivalent. Nothing certified differs.
- "The library carries zero meridional initial velocity and a free z0" (asserted inside E-01/LH-12/P3): refuted. Neither `Theorem01prime_Statement'` nor `Theorem01prime_Statement` has such a clause; the ring is fixed at z = 0 (`Ring/Statement.lean:16` uses `(x 2)`). The library's force torus (radius 3r₀/4 + r₀/10⁷, `Statement.lean:22`) is also larger than the paper's R < r0/2 (E-04).
- "Theorem 1.1 at κ = 1 implies the boussinesq-blowup proposition directly" (inside B-01): refuted as literal; the Lean uniqueness clause is over the Lipschitz class X_τ, so Prop 10.2 is needed. Also the κ rescaling direction is θ ↦ θ/κ, not κθ.
- "boussinesq-blowup's Staircase indicates a different construction" (inside LH-13): refuted; affinecore carries a staircase too (`affinecore/README.md:38`). Which project is the "first solution" of the AI statement cannot be settled from these files.
- "NOTICE copyright and git author are inconsistencies" (inside P6): refuted; copyright holder and author may differ, and Buckmaster co-authors the manuscript.
- "Pressure gauge omission weakens boussinesq-blowup" (inside B-01): refuted; p is existential and only ∇p enters, so p(0,t) = 0 is a normalisation.
- E-03's grep evidence ("lean|formal|claude" matches only line 99 of euler.txt): the regex was off, but the conclusion stands: euler.txt never mentions Lean.
