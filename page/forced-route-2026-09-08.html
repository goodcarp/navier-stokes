The Forced Route

Three forced blowup papers were released on 8 September 2026, with Lean certificates and a four page statement reporting an unseen full Navier–Stokes construction. Two fences, one proved here and one assembled from the partial regularity theory, say that the released mechanism is not the one that gets there.

Release

What happened

On 8 September 2026 Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster posted four documents on Buckmaster’s page at the Courant Institute: a 112 page paper on forced Euler, a 76 page paper on forced Boussinesq, a 57 page paper on incompressible porous media written with Coiculescu, and a four page statement. A Lean repository, fluid_lean, was pushed the same day at a single commit.

The Euler theorem is a finite time blowup for the forced axisymmetric equations with swirl on three dimensional space. The data are smooth and compactly supported in a solid torus away from the axis, the force is smooth in space and time and supported in the same torus, and the solution is smooth up to the blowup time and unique in the locally Lipschitz finite energy class. Circulation and meridional velocity stay bounded. What diverges is the gradient of the circulation and the supremum of the vorticity, whose time integral is infinite. The mechanism is the Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa layered cascade: bounded amplitude, growing derivatives.

The Boussinesq paper runs the same mechanism in two dimensions, with a force that stays smooth through the blowup time, the temperature bounded and its gradient unbounded. The porous media paper is on the two dimensional torus, with a space and time smooth force, gradient blowup, and the density converging in Hölder class for every exponent below one.

The statement makes two further claims. The first is a hypo-dissipative Navier–Stokes blowup whose Lean formalization is unfinished and was not released. The second is reported, not asserted: the statement says a third party told its author that a forced full Navier–Stokes blowup had been produced on three dimensional space and on the torus with a smooth force, which is Fefferman’s options (C) and (D), running to about a hundred pages and prompted in the past few days. That manuscript has not been seen, here or anywhere public.

Two honesty notes belong with the above. A search on the day of the release found nothing posted for the reported full Navier–Stokes claim, so nothing on this page depends on it. And the released Euler paper was not found on arXiv either: the index for the author was current through 2 September 2026 and returned no such paper, so everything said here about the released results is taken from the posted documents and treated as reported rather than as a cited theorem.

Fences

Two fences

Both claims can be tested before anyone reads a hundred pages. What follows are two necessary conditions on any forced blowup for the full equations, with the proof status of each stated plainly. They are conditions on the solution rather than on the construction, so they apply to any route to it. The dissipation is written as the fractional operator of order α, with α equal to two the Navier–Stokes case.

Lemma A · proved here

Bounded velocity continues, for every dissipation order above one.

StatementLet α lie in the interval from one to two, viscosity positive, and let u be the strong solution with smooth divergence free data of finite energy and a force integrable in time with values in L2 and square integrable in time with values in Hs, on an interval reaching past the candidate blowup time. If the supremum of u is bounded up to that time, then the Hs norm is bounded too, and the solution extends past it as a classical solution.

Proof in wordsThe scale invariant Lebesgue exponent is three over α minus one, so the supremum norm is critical exactly at order one and has room to spare above it. Pair the equation with the fractional derivative of the solution, which kills the pressure, bound the nonlinear term by the endpoint Kato–Ponce estimate, and split the derivatives so that Young’s inequality absorbs it into the dissipation. That absorption is available precisely when the order exceeds one. Grönwall closes the bound, and a Kato type local existence argument carries the solution past the time.

ScopeProved in full here for orders above one and up to two, using one external ingredient, the Kato–Ponce product estimate, quoted with its hypotheses checked on both the whole space and the torus. At order two the conclusion is classical and the Clay problem description states it; what is new is the fractional range and the explicit force class. The honest hypothesis is that force class, not the words smooth and bounded: a constant force is smooth and bounded and is not in it.

ConsequenceThe released family keeps the velocity bounded, by construction and in the dissipative paper by conclusion. It is therefore shut out for every order above one, and so at Navier–Stokes, whatever force is chosen, as long as the force lies in that class. The naive ladder from forced Euler to hypo-dissipative to full is closed at the second rung.

Lemma B · assembled from CKN

Axisymmetric full Navier–Stokes singularities lie on the axis.

StatementAt order two, let u be an axisymmetric classical solution with smooth axisymmetric divergence free data of finite energy and a smooth axisymmetric force that is divergence free and lies in Lq for some q above five halves, and let the first singular time be reached. Then every blowup point lies on the axis of symmetry.

Proof in wordsOn backward cylinders inside the smooth region the solution is a suitable weak solution, so the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg regularity criterion applies at every candidate point, and the covering argument gives one dimensional parabolic measure zero for the singular set at that time. Axisymmetry makes the set invariant under rotation about the axis, so a single off-axis singular point would drag an entire circle in with it, and a circle has positive one dimensional measure.

ScopeAssembled from cited theorems, with the rotation invariance step proved here. The localisation itself is a standard remark attributed to Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg and is not claimed as new. On the whole space the first step needs a decay input at spatial infinity, which is a real condition and is flagged rather than papered over. The partial regularity input was verified against a secondary source; the 1982 paper was not accessed directly.

ConsequenceThe fence excludes an off-axis singular set, and not off-axis support of the data and the force: with viscosity the support spreads at once, so the singular point may still sit on the axis. Below order two the same statement degrades by a measured amount, since the Tang and Yu bound gives dimension at most five minus twice the order, which exceeds one everywhere below two. That is exactly why the off-axis torus geometry is available for Euler and in the hypo-dissipative range, and not at Navier–Stokes.

Theorem · refereed 2026-09-08

In the axisymmetric sub-class of Fefferman’s (C), with the solution taken in the strong class (continuous into every Sobolev space up to the singular time, the class the two fences already live in): every point of the blow-up set lies on the axis; the self-similar rate is impossible in the Chen–Strain–Tsai–Yau form, and, under the decay hypothesis Koch, Nadirashvili, Seregin and Šverák themselves use, in their form as well, so the blowup is Type II; and swirl-free data driven by a swirl-free force never break down, so any winning construction carries swirl.

The force enters the rescaling only through Fefferman’s decay condition on it, which is the load-bearing hypothesis; smoothness alone would not do, for the trivial reason Koch and coauthors already record. Every step at which the force appears is written out; the ancient limit that the Liouville theorems judge is genuinely unforced. One more thing came out of the periodic case: a continuous periodic axisymmetric divergence-free field is a constant vertical field, so the axisymmetric sub-class of (D) is trivial and any periodic statement has to be about locally axisymmetric solutions. The theorem was first stated in the strong-solution class. The same evening, the released paper’s comparison lemma was adapted and, with a new proposition that every Clay-class solution has finite dissipation, the theorem was restated in the bare Clay class, with the strong-solution hypothesis deleted; one step rests on the partial-regularity theory through a secondary source and is graded accordingly, and a second, independent line then re-derived the new proposition blind by a different route and sharpened it to an equality. What remains on that theorem is a citation check, not a mathematical gap.

Fefferman’s two options, restatedOption (C), “breakdown of Navier-Stokes solutions” on three dimensional space, asks for a smooth divergence free initial field and a smooth force, both satisfying the problem description’s decay conditions, for which no solution exists that is smooth with bounded energy for all time. Option (D) asks the same in the periodic case, with the force satisfying the periodic decay condition and the solution required to be smooth and periodic, pressure included.

Two features of the pair matter for a cascade. The decay conditions are strong: every space and time derivative of the force has to decay faster than every polynomial rate, uniformly, which a force summed from layers of growing frequency and shrinking support must be checked against jointly and not layer by layer. And both options ask for non-existence in the class, not for one solution that breaks down, so a construction delivers them only together with a uniqueness statement in that class. The problem description itself notes that uniqueness of weak solutions is not known.

Checklist

What a winning construction must show

Six questions, in order. A failure of item 1, of item 3 in the axisymmetric case with the decay input noted above, of item 5 or of item 6 is decisive. Items 2 and 4 are expected but unproved when a force is present, so a failure there is a red flag rather than a refutation. The labels below are the note’s own.

  1. Is the supremum of the velocity bounded up to the blowup time?Decisive

    If it is, the claim is false by Lemma A, provided the force lies in the class that Fefferman’s decay conditions imply. This is the fence that the layered cascade fails, and the fence that the reported Euler mechanism fails. Ask for the claimed growth rate of the supremum explicitly.

  2. Does the L3 norm diverge?Red flag

    Necessary without a force on the whole space, by Escauriaza, Seregin and Šverák and by Seregin’s later paper. Both are stated for the unforced Cauchy problem, and no periodic version is cited, so this is unproved with a force and unproved on the torus. A proof that produces a bounded norm here should be put to its authors rather than dismissed.

  3. Where is the singular point, and is the flow axisymmetric?Decisive

    If it is axisymmetric, every blowup point sits on the axis by Lemma B, so a singular set containing an off-axis point, a circle for instance, is excluded at order two. Off-axis support of the data or the force is not by itself excluded, so ask where the singular point is. Check the force too: in Lq for some q above five halves, and divergence free after Leray projection.

  4. What is the rate?Red flag

    In the axisymmetric case a Type I rate is excluded for the unforced equations only. A claimed forced blowup at that rate must either extend those theorems or explain why the force breaks them, and the decay hypothesis they carry has to be checked for the claimed solution. Leray’s lower bound from the other side is likewise an unforced statement.

  5. Does the energy add up?Decisive

    Bounded energy is never the obstruction: under the force conditions the L2 norm stays bounded on the whole interval, so Fefferman’s energy condition is satisfied for free. The checkable request is the energy budget of the cascade layer by layer, with the sum of the layer energies shown to stay bounded.

  6. Is it actually the Clay problem?Decisive

    Does the force satisfy the decay conditions with the quantifiers honoured jointly, with periodic pressure on the torus? Is the data in the class? Is the conclusion non-existence in the class rather than breakdown of one solution, with the uniqueness step supplied? Is the viscosity fixed throughout, with no vanishing limit, and is the final force a single function of space and time in the class? Data and force may depend on the viscosity, which both options allow.

Ceiling

The ceiling of the layered cascade

The other question is how far the mechanism itself can be pushed if a dissipation term is added to the momentum equation. A scout note works inside the amplitude system of the released papers, under two assumptions stated up front: damping enters as a diagonal decay at the layer’s own frequency, and nothing else in the construction changes. Its results are exponent conditions, reproduced by a numerical toy to four digits. None of them is a theorem, and the second assumption is where the released proof would break first.

The proved value at the top is exactly twenty two minus eight root seven, over nine, or 0.0927, and the force there is rough in time. The one half on the second line is a supremum of the amplitude system and not a construction; reaching it needs a limit that the released correction budget does not permit, on two separate counts.

The three gaps are of different kinds. From 0.0927 to one half is a gap in optimization: both endpoints obey the same inequality, damping at most growth, and both are the same mechanism tuned differently. From one half to one is a gap in mechanism, since closing it needs a growth rate proportional to the frequency rather than to its square root, which a two field Rayleigh–Taylor pair does not give. From one to two needs unbounded velocity, which no construction in this family has.

Taken as written, the released schedules do worse than any line of that table. Both hard-wire a scale separation exponent that grows from stage to stage, so the stage ceiling falls to zero and no fixed positive order survives every stage. The small numbers that come out of the arithmetic, about 1.5 × 10−6 for the Boussinesq schedule and 5.7 × 10−14 for the Euler schedule, are earliest stage values and not schedule ceilings.

A second obstruction has a narrower scope and is worth stating exactly. A smooth force needs the number of uniformly controlled derivatives to grow without bound, and the papers’ own correction budgets cap that number by a fixed fraction of the scale separation exponent, one part in 120 as written for Boussinesq and visibly relaxable to about one in 20, one part in 8192 for Euler. So inside the released correction scheme a smooth force and a fixed positive dissipation order cannot both be had, and a force of finite order buys at most an order inversely proportional to it. The scope: this is a statement about that correction scheme under the two assumptions above, not about all schemes, and for the released schedules it is redundant, because the schedules already send the stage ceiling to zero on their own.

One prediction is on the record, made while the hypo-dissipative paper was still unreleased. It cannot be the released architecture with damping added. Expect either a different scheme or a force of finite regularity.

Certificates

The certificates

The Lean repository holds three projects. Each states its theorem over Mathlib alone in a challenge file, proves it in a solution file, and restricts the permitted axioms to the standard three. There are exactly three deliberate placeholders and no axiom declarations. Thirty-three independent readers went through the projects across four lenses, and every finding that survived carried two independent refutation votes.

The verdict: the three statement files certify sound and non-trivial weakenings of the papers’ headline theorems, not the theorems as printed. No loophole was found. Every cheap object that would satisfy a statement without doing any work, the zero solution, a solution already singular everywhere, junk derivative values, a vacuous integral, an empty competitor class, is excluded by an accompanying clause, and the definitions in the challenge files are text identical to the library originals. Nothing certified is false; the certified propositions are corollaries of the papers’ theorems. What the certificates leave out is undocumented, and that is the finding.

Then the challenge file should be diffed against the clause list above before any headline description of what has been certified is trusted.

Clock

The clock

Everything above is a fence around someone else’s construction. The line this campaign runs itself points the other way: instead of asking what a construction might do, it asks how fast the quantity a blowup needs can actually double.

Theorem · assembled 2026-09-08, modulo eight named items, two since discharged

Take a tapered, mollified vortex ring of amplitude M, shell aspect ratio L equal to the logarithm of outer over inner radius, and inner radius a fixed multiple of the viscous length. For L above an explicit threshold, the first time the vorticity maximum reaches three halves of M satisfies M Td at most c2(1+ε(L))/2L, with c2 = 2 log(3/2)/κδ = 1.649 and ε decreasing to the strain deficit of the mollified profile. In energy Reynolds number the doubling time is at most c2(1+ε)/log Λ once log Λ exceeds 2.85 million on proved constants, or about thirty-five hundred on measured ones, on the third sheet’s certified constants. With the lower bound already on the record, the clock is then pinned on both sides: one over the amplitude times the logarithm of the Reynolds number.

What moved today. The bootstrap that compares the true flow to a reference strain carried an amplification of e34 in the previous assembly, because the reference strain was fixed in advance and its error fed back through a Lipschitz constant. Defining the reference from the true solution’s own exterior strain removes that term as an identity, and the amplification becomes 3.4. The gradient bound off the exact profile, the other block, is proved on all of space by two weighted maximum principles and one Riesz composition, with no logarithm, and with the homogeneity machinery of the earlier record off the critical path. Both pieces went through single referees; the referee reports are in the repository beside the proofs.

Status, exactly. The chain is complete modulo eight named items. One, the approximate-maximum step inside the two maximum principles, is routine and carries most of the others; one, the exact-plateau hypothesis of the viscous lemma, needs a one-line restatement; one is the strong-solution class every note on this page shares. The viscous column of the budget is numerically dead. Granting the list, the family violates the absolute, log-free lower bound of Bradshaw, Farhat and Grujić’s Theorem 10, whose every stated hypothesis it satisfies, so that theorem is false as stated. Later the same day the routine step was written out: the two maximum principles are theorems for the linear problem, the decay and integrability conditions a referee had asked for are proved rather than assumed, and the datum constants are the closed forms 1/sin δ and 1/sin² δ. The viscous restatement is done too and yields a constraint, not a cost: the tracked point must sit deep in the plateau, and the threshold moves by one part in eighty six thousand. Two technical items remain before the contradiction is asserted. A review from the other line, written blind to this one, then added five consistency items the assembly had missed: the estimates are proved on a window shorter than the concluded time, the slope-match used to import the second-derivative bound is wrong, that bound is imported on a narrower strain range than the cap allows, two sampled scans are labelled proved, and a failed iteration is called a nonexistence threshold. All five were repaired by evening, and a second referee on the repaired sheet found five more, every one a bounded repair, the largest a ball tangent to the taper cone. The third sheet moved the ball four mollification widths off the cone, replaced every sampled constant on the proved branch with an enclosure, and put the reach at a shell aspect ratio of 1.42 million on proved constants against 1,762 measured. Its referee, overnight, graded it not proved as stated for three reasons, none of which moves a number: the angular hypothesis had been checked by a grid lower bound doubled, and the referee replaced it with a certified upper bound of 0.203 against the 0.262 the sheet assumes; the continuation step rested on a sentence that is false in exactly the case the argument lives in, and needs a dichotomy on the window up to the maximal time; and the statement quantified over an unbounded range of the outer parameter on which its own hypothesis fails. The fourth sheet, in progress, writes the dichotomy as a lemma, bounds the range, and tags every reach number with the seven constants and the one cited estimate it still rests on. The clock is written end to end, with its remaining items enumerated, which is more than most published clocks can say.

Scorecard

The release, scored

At six in the evening the outside lab published: a 165 page paper claiming Fefferman’s (C) and (D) with a smooth force compactly supported in space and time, from rest, with a Lean certificate in the same comparator layout as the morning’s release, and a second paper claiming unforced Euler blowup from smooth compactly supported data. Thirty-six independent readers went through both papers and the certificate against the checklist above, with one refuter on every finding.

The construction walks through every door the fences name. The velocity is unbounded with the explicit rate τ−1/2−h for h below one hundredth, so Lemma A does not bite. The singular set is one space-time point at the origin, on the axis, though the full flow is not axisymmetric because the pulses carry angular frequencies, so Lemma B and the Type II theorem do not bind; the profile is nonetheless on-axis, Type II by the margin τ−h, and swirl-carrying, which is the corridor those theorems predicted. The L3 norm diverges like τ−4h/3. The energy is bounded by a lemma proved from the equation. The force satisfies Fefferman’s decay condition trivially. And the uniqueness step that our own notes said every such theorem was missing is present, as their Lemma 10.5, against any smooth competitor with bounded energy, with no enstrophy and no decay asked of the competitor. Every decisive item on the checklist passes on text we could locate.

No fatal or major defect was found in either paper by a bounded audit. The one surviving major finding is about the certificate’s coverage, and it is the same pattern as the morning: the certified Navier–Stokes statements are Fefferman’s (C) and (D) verbatim, copied from an independent formal-conjectures file, and the paper’s distinctive clauses, the compact support of velocity and pressure, the bounded energy, and the unbounded velocity itself, appear in no certified statement, while the energy lemma is proved and never called. No hash binds the papers to the repository. The alternative (D) is proved for competitors whose pressure is periodic, which is the Clay erratum’s reading and is stated openly. The three joints a referee should open first are named with page numbers in the audit: the profile existence theorem with its stress-cone margin, the induction whose margins close at seventeen hundredths, and the analyticity forced at the axis.

Two things follow for the work on this page. Their uniqueness lemma discharges the strong-solution hypothesis in our Type II theorem, which was the largest open item in that stack; it is being imported now. And the unforced problem, which their Euler paper reaches by building the layers into the limiting datum, does not cross to viscosity by that route, so the strained-core line is no longer a race with anyone.

Construction

The strained core

The other half of the day ran as a second, independent line, on the constructive side of the same problem: ordinary unforced Navier–Stokes at viscosity one over a thousand, nine passes between two and eight in the morning, every pass with a report, a verification note, an independent audit and its own check scripts. Its first finding was the two fences above, reached on its own: the released Euler velocity cannot be reused because bounded velocity gives a uniform gradient bound and continuation, and a suitable axisymmetric solution cannot carry an off-axis singular point. From there it stopped transferring and started building.

What it holds, in its own words. A compact smooth datum, one fixed field, for which the initial pressure feedback of a rotating strained core is certified by outward-rounded interval arithmetic, with the gate value enclosed below minus thirty three and the core ratio acceleration above sixteen. A numerical lead, reproduced here to the digit, in which the pressure derivative that decides whether strain grows against rotation sits near minus two hundred and forty one against a threshold of zero, stable to one part in a thousand under refinement. A finite transfer theorem: on one actual solution, every rotating test mode inside the initial rigid core gains by an explicit factor, and one smaller receiver can be chosen so that velocity, local Reynolds number and local energy scale with exponents that match a candidate budget at a = 1/4. An exact affine model of the whole leading envelope with a certified energy gain above fifteen percent. And an explicit next datum with a strict initial core cone and audited initial derivatives.

What it does not hold, also in its own words: no persistence interval for the compact three dimensional field, no inherited return of the evolved state to a class it can restart from, no repeated stage, and no blowup. The return problem is now explicit. The evolved field carries a symmetric strain of first order in time that no isotropic rescaling removes, so the finite gain does not bring the field back to the rigid core. That is the same block the clock line met this morning under another name, and the fix that worked there, defining the reference from the evolved state so the mismatch is a coordinate rather than an error, is the direction its own status note already names.

Two things are worth saying about where this sits. Its exponent window is exactly the one the fences allow: velocity per stage scaling like q−(1+a) with advective stage times makes the velocity run faster than the self-similar rate precisely when a is positive, finite energy needs a below one half, and a = 1/4 sits in the middle. And every one of its hundred check scripts was re-run on a second machine this morning, ninety four executed and ninety four passed, with the interval certificates of five passes agreeing bit for bit; the three mismatches found are a wording revision and two grid index picks near a sign crossing, none a number. The elementary core of the line is in Lean as well: the two coefficients of the core pressure, eighteen sevenths and two fifths, are theorems for every cutoff profile in the stated class, since the profile moments cancel under integration by parts, and the receiver exponent identities and the Type II window are checked against Mathlib with the standard axioms only, alongside the cores of the two fences.

Method

On method

Everything on this page came through the same pipeline. A reading or a proof, then adversarial review by models that saw neither the author’s reasoning nor each other’s work, then a written grade against a rubric pinned before the run. The two fence notes went through three lenses and two lenses and absorbed twenty and sixteen corrections between them, each correction re-checked against the source before it was applied. The certificate audit took thirty-three independent readers and required two independent refutations for every surviving finding.

Sources were read at source: the Clay problem description, the arXiv records and the author copies of the papers behind each citation, the released documents, and the Lean projects, which were read and not built. Where a source could not be reached, the note says so and names the secondary source it used instead. Grades are ours; the referee reports are how they were earned. This is not a solution of the Millennium problem. It is the map of what one has to be.
