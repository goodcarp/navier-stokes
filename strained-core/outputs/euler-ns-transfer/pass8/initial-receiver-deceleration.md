# Actual initial deceleration of the packet-center receiving maximum

For the unchanged pass7 family, at every amplitude 3/4<=lambda<=1,
the positive mean angular momentum initially grows at the packet-center
receiver but its growth strongly decelerates. The complete pressure is included:

\[
 \boxed{85<G_t(0,r_*,\pm4)<186,\qquad
 G_{tt}(0,r_*,\pm4)<-250000.}
 \tag{1}
\]

The unique initial radial maximizing radius is certified in

\[
 \frac{1077377876667}{5000000000000}
 <r_*<
 \frac{1077377876668}{5000000000000}.
 \tag{2}
\]

These are exact rational endpoints, approximately 0.2154755753334 and
0.2154755753336. The radius is independent of lambda because the positive
outer initial mean is A_lambda times the same radial profile.

## Exact initial second derivative

Write g(r)=r² C(r), C(r)=chi(r²/a²), a=63/200, m=4, k=20,
c=7/5, nu=1/1000, and T0=lambda²mk/(2r). At z=4, the seed and
axial swirl envelopes are flat in a neighborhood of the receiver.
The [full covariance calculation](covariance-evolution.md) proves

\[
 G_{tt}=A_\lambda L_2g+
 \frac{\lambda^2m^2 A_\lambda g''}{2r^2}
 +\nu T_0\left(\frac{2m^2+4}{r^2}-2k^2\right)
 +\frac{\lambda^2 A_\lambda}{2}
 \operatorname{Re}\!\left\{e^{-ikr}
   \left[\left(k^2r+\frac{m^2}r+ik\right)p_{m,r}-ikr s_m\right]\right\},
 \tag{3}
\]

all evaluated at r=r*, z=4, where

\[
 L_2g=c^2r^2g''-2c\nu r g'''+
 \nu^2(g''''-2g'''/r+3g''/r^2).
\]

Here p_m is the actual unit m=4 pressure generated by the outer swirl
and the seed, and s_m=-Delta_m p_m. Both radial and newly generated axial
covariance fluxes were retained before using the Poisson equation to obtain
(3). Discarding the axial pressure flux would give a different second jet.
The local mean acceleration multiplies the initial gradient of G, which
vanishes at this receiver; this is why its separate mean pressure does not
appear in (3).

At a maximizing radius, V0'=-C for V0=rC, and the source simplifies to

\[
 e^{-ikr}s_m=2C(m^2/r^2-k^2-ik/r).
\]

In particular its real contribution to the last term in (3) is
-lambda² A_lambda k² C; its sign is not discarded.

## Whole-space pressure control, without a finite box

The exact horizontal trial from pass7 is P(r)F(z). On this axial plateau
F=1. The [harmonic-slab bound](covariance-evolution-pressure-slab.md) proves

\[
 p_{m,r}(r_*,4)=P'(r_*)+\epsilon,\qquad |\epsilon|<5.
 \tag{4}
\]

This is a bound on the full complex amplitude, derived from the existing
outward residual norm and harmonicity in the whole infinite slab
|z-4|<9/40. It includes both packets, the pressure generated by the endcaps,
and the complete radial tails.

The derivative of the exact radial Green formula is

\[
 P'=-40iC e^{20ir}+12r^{-5}I_+-20r^3I_-,
\]
\[
 I_+=\int_0^r t^4 C'(t)e(t)e^{20it}\,dt,\qquad
 I_-=\int_r^\infty t^{-4}C'(t)e(t)e^{20it}\,dt.
 \tag{5}
\]

Every local C'e term cancels in this derivative. One-dimensional range
integration encloses these moments directly; numerical differentiation of
a pressure grid is unnecessary.

The new interval program first certifies uniqueness of the radial maximum:
g'>0 below 1/5 in the previously established maximizing range, g'<0 above
23/100, g''<0 throughout [1/5,23/100], and opposite g' signs at (2).
It then encloses cutoff derivatives through order four and both Green
moments at (2), using 2048 outward panels at 40 decimal digits.

The mean-transport coefficient L2g, the complete seed coefficient after
adding the worst-case error (4), and the remaining viscous-torque factor
are all negative. Thus using A_lambda>780 and lambda²>=9/16 gives a valid
upper bound, and discarding the negative viscous-torque term enlarges it.
The saved upper endpoint is less than -293333, which proves (1).
The displayed interval encloses an upper-bound expression; it is not a
two-sided interval for the actual Gtt.

## Radial recentering does not remove this initial deceleration

At fixed z=4, the strict initial radial maximum continues to a smooth
local radial critical branch r_lambda(t). Set
H_lambda(t)=G_lambda(t,r_lambda(t),4).
The implicit-function identities are

\[
 \dot r_\lambda(0)=-\frac{G_{tr}}{G_{rr}},\qquad
 H_\lambda''(0)=G_{tt}-\frac{G_{tr}^2}{G_{rr}},
\]
\[
 G_{tr}=A_\lambda[-crg''+\nu(g'''-g''/r)]-T_0/r,\qquad
 G_{rr}=A_\lambda g''<0.
\]

The second term in H'' is a positive correction and must be included.
The same interval calculation bounds it above by 1875 and proves

\[
 \boxed{H_\lambda''(0)<-240000,\qquad
        \dot r_\lambda(0)>1/5>0.}
 \tag{6}
\]

Thus the packet-center radial maximum initially expands and decelerates.
This is the actual initial critical branch, not a prescribed material ray.

The initial global maximum has a flat axial plateau. Another axial location
may be selected at positive time, so (6) is not a second-derivative bound
for the unrestricted global maximum. Nor does a negative initial second
derivative prove a turning time: higher jets can change it rapidly.

For example, only under an additional bound H''<=-240000 throughout a
specified interval would H'(0)<186 imply the conditional slope estimate
H'(t)<186-240000t. No such positive-time curvature bound is proved here.
The earlier strictly positive local gain remains valid.

The constructive consequence is to require evolved covariance, receiver
geometry and replenishment in the next stage estimate. Passive winding
or an unchanged receiver cannot supply that estimate by itself.
