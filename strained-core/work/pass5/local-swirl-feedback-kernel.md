# Derivative-free local swirl feedback on the annular pump plateau

Use cylindrical coordinates \((r,\theta,z)\), with \(r\) the cylindrical radius, and put \(R^2=r^2+z^2\). Suppose the exterior swirl \(v=w(r,z)e_\theta\) is compactly supported entirely where the radial annular pump is the affine field
\[
m=c(re_r-2ze_z).
\]
All expressions below are exact initial cubic coefficients in the audited mixed pressure functional. The pressure response of the annular pump itself is a separate term.

The two required kernels are
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_\theta&=\frac{3(4z^2-r^2)}{4\pi R^7},\\
K_{\rm loc}&=-\frac{45(r^4-12r^2z^2+8z^4)}{4\pi R^9},\\
S_\theta&=-\frac{45(r^4-12r^2z^2+8z^4)}{4\pi R^{11}}
                 =\frac{K_{\rm loc}}{R^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Writing \(t=r^2/z^2\), define
\[
F(t)=\frac{15(t^2-12t+8)}{(4-t)(1+t)},\qquad
G(t)=\frac{F(t)}{1+t}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\frac{K_{\rm loc}}{Q_\theta}=-F(t),\qquad
       \frac{S_\theta}{Q_\theta}=-\frac{G(t)}{z^2}.}
\tag{2}
\]
These ratios are smooth on the selected packet support and permit bounds using \(C_v=\int Q_\theta w^2\,dx>0\) as a normalization, without first computing \(C_v\) exactly.

## Derivation of the local cubic contribution

Let \(D=r\partial_r-2z\partial_z\). The part of the initial non-pressure acceleration generated by the swirl is
\[
(u_1)_r\supset \frac{w^2}{r},\qquad
(u_1)_\theta\supset-c(Dw+w).
\]
The first term is the centrifugal meridional response; the second includes the cylindrical connection term. Pairing both with the exterior datum in \(-2\int Q_{ij}(u_o)_i(u_1)_j\), the coefficient of \(cw^2\) is
\[
-2c\int\left(Q_{rr}-\frac{2z}{r}Q_{rz}\right)w^2
+2c\int Q_\theta w(Dw+w).
\tag{3}
\]
The vector field \(re_r-2ze_z\) has zero three-dimensional divergence. Compact support therefore gives
\(\int Q_\theta D(w^2)=-\int(DQ_\theta)w^2\).
Thus (3) is exactly
\[
c\int K_{\rm loc}w^2,\qquad
K_{\rm loc}=-DQ_\theta+2Q_\theta
                  -2\left(Q_{rr}-\frac{2z}{r}Q_{rz}\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Using \(Q_{ij}=-\partial_{zzij}N\), direct differentiation gives
\[
Q_{rr}=\frac{3(4r^4-27r^2z^2+4z^4)}{4\pi R^9},
\quad
Q_{rz}=-\frac{15rz(4z^2-3r^2)}{4\pi R^9}.
\]
Substitution proves (1). Smooth axisymmetric swirl has \(w=O(r)\), so the cylindrical expressions extend regularly across the axis. Both terms in (3) are essential; retaining only swirl transport would calculate a different coefficient.

## Exact core response to the outer swirl

Since \(v\) is solenoidal and supported away from the core,
\[
p[v]=N*\partial_i\partial_j(v_iv_j).
\]
Differentiating four times in \(z\), integrating by parts, and taking the azimuthal Hessian entry gives
\[
p[v]_{zzzz}(0)=
\int\left(\frac1r\partial_r\partial_z^4N\right)w^2\,dx
=\int S_\theta w^2\,dx.
\tag{5}
\]
There is no contact term on this exterior support. Also \(p[v]_{zz}(0)=-C_v\).
The exact core response proved in core-exterior-pressure-response.md consequently becomes
\[
\mathcal C(-\nabla p[v])
=b\int Q_\theta w^2
           \left[-\frac{18}{7}+\frac{M_\psi G(t)}{15z^2}\right]dx,
\quad M_\psi=\int_0^\infty\psi(s)\,ds.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the complete coefficient proportional to \(bA^2\) is the weighted average in (6). No other \(bA^2\) interaction survives the initial disjoint supports.

For the symmetric smooth cutoff actually used in the candidate, \(\psi=1\) on \(s\le1/4\), \(\psi=0\) on \(s\ge1\), and its transition satisfies
\(\psi(s)+\psi(5/4-s)=1\). Hence
\[
\boxed{M_\psi=\frac14+\frac12\left(1-\frac14\right)=\frac58.}
\tag{7}
\]
This is an exact symmetry identity, not a quadrature estimate.

## Uniform rational bounds for the selected packet

Both \(F\) and \(G\) are positive and decreasing on \(0\le t\le1/16\):
\[
F'(t)=-\frac{45(3t^2-8t+24)}{(4-t)^2(1+t)^2}<0,
\quad
G'(t)=\frac{15(t^3-25t^2+80t-104)}{(4-t)^2(1+t)^3}<0.
\tag{8}
\]
The first numerator is \(3(t-4/3)^2+56/3\). The second is at most
\((1/16)^3+5-104<0\) on this interval.

For any support satisfying \(0\le t\le T\le1/16\) and
\(z_{\min}\le|z|\le z_{\max}\), positivity of \(Q_\theta w^2\) gives
\[
-30\le\frac{\int K_{\rm loc}w^2}{C_v}\le-F(T),
\tag{9}
\]
and, when \(M_\psi\ge0\),
\[
-\frac{18}{7}+\frac{M_\psi G(T)}{15z_{\max}^2}
\le
\frac{\mathcal C(-\nabla p[v])}{bC_v}
\le
-\frac{18}{7}+\frac{2M_\psi}{z_{\min}^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (10) is interpreted as the coefficient after removing \(b\), so it remains well-defined at \(b=0\).

The actual profile uses
\[
r\le\frac{63}{200},\quad
\frac{71}{20}\le|z|\le\frac{89}{20},\quad
T=\left(\frac{63}{710}\right)^2=\frac{3969}{504100}.
\]
With \(M_\psi=5/8\), the exact endpoints are
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(T)&=\frac{30134114372415}{1022453805739}
                  =29.4723479958\ldots,\\
L_b&=-\frac{72293765997170443165798}{28803445800475159076977}
                  =-2.5098999091\ldots,\\
U_b&=-\frac{87238}{35287}=-2.4722419021\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore the local pump-plus-core part of the \(A^2\) contribution is bounded above, for \(b,c\ge0\), by
\[
\boxed{A^2C_v\,[\,U_b b-F(T)c\,].}
\tag{12}
\]
At exact neutral tuning for this radial annular pump,
\[
A^2C_v=\frac{38}{7}b^2+\frac25\Omega^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Substituting (13) into (12) removes the normalization integral from this bound. The remaining \(cA^2\) pressure response of the annular pump must still be included before concluding anything about the full gate. Positive viscosity contributes \(\nu d_vA^2\), which likewise needs its own bound.

## The annular response to radial core-swirl pressure is zero

Let the core swirl be any smooth compact radial profile
\(W=h(R^2)re_\theta\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
g_W=\operatorname{tr}[(\nabla W)^2]
       =-2h^2-4r^2hh'.
\tag{14}
\]
Its only angular modes are \(0,2\). Its monopole moment is zero: after angular averaging, the radial integrand is a multiple of
\[
\sqrt{s}\left[-2h^2-\frac83s hh'\right]
       =-\frac43\frac{d}{ds}\left(s^{3/2}h^2\right),
\]
whose endpoint terms vanish. Therefore outside the core
\[
p[W](R,\mu)=B_2R^{-3}P_2(\mu).
\tag{15}
\]
For an annular strain \(S_\eta\), the exact pressure-response weight from radial-annular-pressure-cutoff.md has
\[
q_2(R)=-\frac{32}{7}R^2[3\eta'(R^2)-2R^2\eta''(R^2)].
\]
Its pairing with (15) is proportional to
\[
\int_0^\infty R^{-6}q_2(R)\,dR
=-\frac{32}{7}
 \left[3\int R^{-4}\eta'(R^2)\,dR
             -2\int R^{-2}\eta''(R^2)\,dR\right]=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, since \(\eta\) vanishes near zero and infinity,
\[
\int R^{-2}\eta''(R^2)\,dR
=\frac12\int s^{-3/2}\eta''(s)\,ds
=\frac34\int s^{-5/2}\eta'(s)\,ds
=\frac32\int R^{-4}\eta'(R^2)\,dR.
\]
On the annular support the acceleration generated by \(W\) is only its pressure gradient, so (16) proves the exact full coefficient
\[
\boxed{t_{Wc}=-2\Pi(S_\eta,B(W,W))=0.}
\tag{17}
\]
This cancellation uses a radial annular strain outside a compact radial swirl. It does not remove the annular interaction with the nonradial outer swirl packet.
